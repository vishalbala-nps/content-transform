# Ransomware gang claims it has "the lot" after Sunridge Hospitals hack

By Anjali Rao, Northgate Tech Review. 22 August 2026.

Well, this is awkward. Sunridge Hospitals, the private chain that spent most of last year advertising itself as "India's most digital hospital group", has spent the past four days running on paper.

The chain, which operates 14 hospitals, confirmed on Thursday that a "cyber incident" took its patient records, billing and lab systems offline on 18 August. Ambulances were diverted away from the emergency departments of 3 of its hospitals for roughly 36 hours while staff switched to handwritten charts.

A ransomware group calling itself CinderLock has since claimed responsibility on its leak site, which is hosted at cinderlock-leaks.example. The group says it stole 1.2 TB of data, including records for about 410,000 patients, and has posted what it says are sample files.

Sunridge disputes that. "We have found no evidence at this stage that patient clinical data was taken," chief executive Vikram Sethi said in a statement. "Our priority is patient care, and all 14 hospitals are admitting patients." The company has not said whether it will pay.

Two people familiar with the negotiations told Northgate Tech Review that CinderLock is demanding $4 million. We have not been able to verify that figure, and Sunridge declined to comment on it.

So how did they get in? According to Meera Kulkarni, a threat researcher at the security firm Ashgrove Labs, the attack started with a phishing email sent to finance staff in late July. The email linked to a fake payroll login page at sunridge-payroll.example. Kulkarni says the attackers then sat inside the network for more than three weeks before deploying ransomware.

Honestly, you would think a hospital chain in 2026 would have multi-factor authentication on its payroll logins. Sunridge says it is "reviewing its controls".

The State Health Data Protection Authority said it has opened an investigation and that Sunridge notified it within the 72 hours the law requires.

What should patients do? Sunridge says patients will be contacted directly if their data is found to be affected. Until then, the Authority advises Sunridge patients to be wary of calls or messages asking for payment or medical details, and to confirm any request by calling the hospital on its published number.
