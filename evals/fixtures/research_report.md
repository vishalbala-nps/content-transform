# Rooftop Solar Among Small Businesses: 2026 Survey Findings

Meridian Energy Policy Institute. Research report, July 2026.

## Summary

Rooftop solar adoption among small businesses has grown quickly, but most of the remaining demand is held back by upfront cost and slow grid approvals rather than by lack of interest. Of the businesses surveyed, 23% now have rooftop solar installed, up from 14% in our 2024 survey.

## Method

We surveyed 1,850 small businesses with fewer than 50 employees across 12 cities between February and April 2026. Responses were collected by telephone and weighted by sector and city size. Figures are self-reported.

## Key findings

Businesses that installed solar report a median payback period of 4.6 years. Among businesses without solar, 58% said they would install it within two years if the barriers below were removed.

Upfront cost was the most common barrier, cited by 61% of businesses without solar. Among businesses that rent their premises, 38% said they could not get their landlord's permission. Delays in net-metering approval were cited by 27%.

The average time from application to net-metering approval was 94 days, against the 30 days set by regulation. Only 1 in 5 applications was approved within the 30-day limit.

Adoption varies sharply by sector:

| Sector | Share with rooftop solar |
|---|---|
| Manufacturing | 34% |
| Cold storage and food processing | 31% |
| Retail | 17% |
| Hospitality | 15% |
| Professional services | 9% |

## Recommendations

- Create a single-window online approval for rooftop solar and net metering, with automatic approval if the utility does not respond within 30 days.
- Offer on-bill financing so that businesses repay installation costs through their electricity bills.
- Publish a standard landlord consent template that separates the solar installation from the lease.

## Limitations

The survey covers urban businesses only and relies on self-reported costs and savings. It does not measure the quality of installations or actual generation.
